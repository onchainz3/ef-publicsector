Where Ethereum can contribute

Explore how Ethereum can support public services, coordination between organizations, and community decision-making. The use cases below outline possible approaches and the implementation choices they would involve.

Use cases for the public interest

Explore how institutions and communities could use Ethereum for a variety of purposes, such as overseeing public work, verifying information, and coordinating services. Adapted from research by the Ethereum Foundation Use Case Lab, these use cases describe possible approaches and their implementation considerations. They are starting points for exploration, not reports of completed projects.

  • Overseeing public procurement

    • A shared record could connect procurement decisions and approvals across organizations
    • Agencies, suppliers, and auditors could review contract awards and delivery without relying solely on one organization’s records

  • Confirming eligibility while minimizing data collection

    • Verify specific requirements (e.g. minimum age) while limiting the personal information disclosed
    • Support data minimization without changing existing eligibility rules

  • Supporting participatory budgeting

    • Connect project proposals, voting results, allocations, and payments in a shared record
    • Residents and public institutions could verify how input was recorded and trace how selected projects were funded

  • Tracing medicines through supply chains

    • Link product identifiers to a shared record of transfers across the supply chain
    • Health authorities and care providers could verify a medicine’s recorded origin and movement

  • Coordinating construction permits

    • Share records of permit submissions, inspections, and decisions across agencies
    • Applicants and officials could track progress, identify pending actions, and review the basis for decisions

  • Tracking climate commitments

    • Bring together climate commitments, reported milestones, and assessments in a shared record
    • Institutions and the public could follow reported progress over time

Building blocks for digital infrastructure

Privacy, accountability, and cooperation depend on how digital services are designed. Explore the various mechanisms in Ethereum that can help institutions turn these principles into practical benefits for the people they serve.

Outcomes, requirements, and Ethereum mechanisms
Public benefit Institutional requirement Ethereum mechanism
Public benefit Coordination across institutions Institutional requirement Carry out actions when agreed-upon conditions are met, while reducing manual handoffs Ethereum mechanism Smart contracts Programs that run on Ethereum and can check submitted requests against conditions written into their code. When those conditions are met, they can automatically carry out specified actions. The results are recorded on Ethereum for participants to verify independently. View use case
Public benefit Private verification Institutional requirement Confirm identity, eligibility, or compliance while collecting only the information needed Ethereum mechanism Zero-knowledge proofs A cryptographic method for proving that a statement is true without revealing the private information used to establish it. A program on Ethereum can verify the proof without receiving those personal details. View use case
Public benefit Verifiable official records Institutional requirement Check who issued an official record and whether it has changed Ethereum mechanism Attestations Digitally signed statements that can be recorded on Ethereum together with a cryptographic hash of a document. A cryptographic hash is a short code derived from the document. Others can check which account signed the statement and whether the document has changed since its hash was recorded on Ethereum, without publishing the document itself. View use case
Public benefit Confidential, auditable transactions Institutional requirement Allow authorized review without making transaction details public Ethereum mechanism Viewing keys Cryptographic keys that give designated parties read-only access to private transaction details, without allowing them to move the funds.
Public benefit More private payments Institutional requirement Protect payment recipients’ privacy by limiting public visibility into their transaction history Ethereum mechanism Stealth addresses Create a new address for each payment, making it harder for outside observers to connect multiple payments to the same recipient.
