Connecting institutional needs with technical expertise

Technical Foundations is a team at the Ethereum Foundation that helps institutions evaluate the technical suitability of Ethereum for digital public infrastructure and other public-interest applications. The team also develops selected proofs of concept and reference implementations, extracting reusable design patterns, software components, and technical guidance.

Technical assessment

Conduct technical due diligence on proposed architectures and claims, and develop technical evaluation frameworks to support informed institutional decisions.

Vendor recommendations and project accreditation fall outside the team's scope.

System requirements and architecture

Translate institutional needs into technical specifications, system designs, and threat models, providing a clear technical foundation for proposed Ethereum-based approaches.

Proofs of concept and reference implementations

Develop selected proofs of concept and reference implementations to demonstrate how Ethereum can support digital public infrastructure, while extracting reusable patterns, software tools, and technical guidance.

This work doesn't extend to delivering bespoke one-off solutions or operating systems in production.

Technical resources and guidance

Browse technical articles, practical tools, demonstrations, and lessons from Technical Foundations’ work. These resources share technical knowledge and practical insights into how Ethereum can support digital public infrastructure and other public-interest applications.

  • Introduction to the Ethereum Stack (opens in a new tab)

    Understand how applications, client APIs, nodes, and smart contracts fit together when integrating Ethereum into a software system.

  • Zero-Knowledge Proofs (opens in a new tab)

    Learn how cryptographic proofs can support verification without disclosing underlying information, including applications to identity and privacy.

  • Decentralized Identity and Private Payments on Ethereum

    A proof of concept, built with requirements from humanitarian aid organisations, that pairs private credentials with private payments on a public network.

  • Private Credential Verification with Issuer Hiding and Revocation

    Two registries on Ethereum that let a zero-knowledge proof show a credential's issuer is approved and the credential is not revoked, without naming the issuer.

  • Benchmarking Zero-knowledge Credential Proofs on Low-end Mobile Devices

    We measured zero-knowledge credential proofs on ten Android phones, from 4 GB low-end models to flagships, to see which proofs fit which phones.

  • Semaphore Paymaster: Private Transaction Sponsorship

    An ERC-4337 paymaster that pays fees for any member of a Semaphore group without learning which member sent the operation.
